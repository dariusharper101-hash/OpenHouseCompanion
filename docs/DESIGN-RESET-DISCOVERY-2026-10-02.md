# Hop design reset — discovery, not an approved design brief

## Why reset
The creator is a designer and rejected the current result as insufficiently considered and too generic for Hop. They specifically asked us to design for the people arriving, the cues that guide them, and the visuals that capture attention. Previous layout, font and color selections are proposals, not approved brand requirements. Build/test passes establish implementation health, not design quality. The earlier craft pass did not complete Impeccable's visual iteration requirement.

## Confirmed inputs
- Hop is Darius Harper / Hop In Real Estate, serving Dallas–Fort Worth.
- Two September 30 real-estate transcripts describe buyers, sellers, renters, investors and people relocating. Family-trust work is separate.
- Useful information should precede contact capture. Buyer preferences should support a shortlist conversation, lender handoff and an owned next step. Renting requires the actual property's application process. Visitor sign-in has a focused purpose.
- Preserve the supplied original white logo/icon artwork. Remove awkward logo containers, give the header considered spacing and make home imagery meaningful.
- The creator wants a distinctive result and a coherent design system before expanding across surfaces.

## What remains unconfirmed
- The brand's desired emotional impression and the characteristics that distinguish Hop personally.
- Which audience and entry source deserve priority on the initial screen. The previous buying-first CTA is an implementation choice, not evidence of audience priority.
- Visual references, color palette and typography. Charcoal/off-white and Bodoni/Public Sans are not approved requirements.
- Available authentic local photography, home-tour imagery, portrait treatment and permission to publish specific property images.

## Source-based diagnosis
This diagnosis comes from current source and creator feedback; no fresh rendered-page inspection is claimed.

1. **Weak positioning.** “A place to call home. A plan to get there.” could introduce almost any real-estate agent. The first screen needs a specific, supportable promise and a reason to choose Hop.
2. **Illustration has replaced art direction.** A stock house image establishes the category but not DFW, Hop's point of view or the customer's desired life. Adding images alone does not solve the creative problem. Existing photos must not be represented as local/current listings.
3. **Equal emphasis conceals priorities.** Six navigation destinations, a buying-first hero, four subsequent routes, an investor link and another tools/areas/visits section expose the implementation structure. Audience priority and intended entry journey should establish the hierarchy.
4. **Repeated choices dilute the flow.** Area comparison, education and conversation invitations recur without a clear progression from curiosity to confidence to action.
5. **Identity arrives late.** Hop's portrait and generic conversation paragraph appear after the customer choices. The brief needs to decide how the person, expertise and local perspective earn trust early.
6. **Tokens lack a creative rationale.** Shared spacing and colors improve consistency but don't establish a recognizable brand. Type, composition, image selection, voice and interaction must express the same idea.

## Customer needs to carry into discovery
These are transcript-grounded contexts to validate, not a claim of completed user research.

| Visitor | Immediate uncertainty | Useful first outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer | What fits my life and comfortable cost? | Understand the search and identify a next step before contact entry. |
| Renter | Can I find something suitable in time, and how do I apply? | Clarify preferences and the property-specific application path. |
| Relocating visitor | Which places fit my commute and daily life? | Narrow area choices around real destinations and constraints. |
| Seller | What would selling involve, and who will help? | Understand the preparation/conversation process without invented valuation. |
| Investor | Is this opportunity suitable and what needs verification? | Establish the analysis questions and a responsible conversation. |
| Open-house visitor | Why am I signing in and how long will this take? | Complete a short, understandable visit entry. |

## Design-system brief to establish next
- A supportable positioning statement and an explicit primary audience/entry journey.
- A visual concept with references and reasons for the image, type and color choices.
- Image roles: authentic place, attainable home, human identity and factual property details.
- Hierarchy: one intended next action with clear alternatives, rather than every feature competing equally.
- Shared rules for logo treatment, typography, spacing, interaction cues and voice.
- Distinct reading/discovery and focused task modes within the same identity.
- Default, empty, image failure, form error, loading and completion behavior.
- One complete first-touch experience to validate at desktop and phone sizes before propagation.
